FOURSCORE — five art directions

Each one answers four questions differently: what is the board, where is it, where does the camera stand, where does the Win95 chrome live. Palette is a consequence of the answer, never the proposal.

Every card opens fullscreen. All local, no server. They compose — 01's world with 02's chrome is a legal order.

01THE LANE SCREEN

The game is the monitor's own animation. A place, not a void: checkered floor, horizon, and the board standing on the ground with a reflection. Chrome type flies at the lens; the HUD is a scoring strip.

Keeps: sunset heat, rockets, shouting chrome. Risk: reads synthwave if it drifts — it has to stay bowling-software, not outrun.

02BOARD.EXE

The game never leaves the operating system. The board is a window, the flames are a screensaver, the rocket has escaped a window and nobody comments. No 3D anywhere — possession is the medium.

Keeps: Win95 as the entire world, flames, rockets. Risk: gives up the "genuinely beautiful" half; drama must come from the OS degrading.

03THE MONUMENT

The goth half taken seriously: the board at the scale of an eclipse, molten discs, camera at ankle height, and one tiny beige window that refuses to be impressed.

Keeps: Win95 as the small absurd intruder; heat lives in the discs. Risk: solemn — if the collision stops being funny it's a metal album cover.

04COSMIC CARPET

Blacklight bowling carpet. Cartoon content, physical material: the print sits in the pile and goes ragged, the neon has a dying letter, the lighting is uneven because the fixtures are cheap. Gritty, not cartoony.

Keeps: rockets, WordArt energy, a beige window floating over the print. Risk: grit is a budget — every element needs a material answer (pile, tube, scuff) or it flattens back into a poster.

05THE BREAK ROOM

A real toy in a real room, shot with a flash. The fever is environmental: the window doesn't look onto anywhere, the TV plays the chrome, management left a note.

Keeps: Win95 (on the TV), the toy's honest plastic. Risk: diorama detail is expensive, and the room has to stay boring for the wrongness to read.
